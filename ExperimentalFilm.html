Total Banani is a collaborative stop-motion short film exploring depression and its impact on everyday life. Through a handmade character and miniature set, we visualised the gradual progression of depression and the often unseen struggles that come with it. The project was developed over four months in Stuttgart.

Initial Design

After forming groups, we were asked to develop a story based on a shared life experience or event that had shaped us in some way. Through group discussions, we explored our experiences with COVID-19 and mental health, eventually developing a concept centred on depression and its impact on everyday life.

We chose stop-motion as our medium because it was a new technique for most of us. We developed a simple, expressive character and decided to use a handmade miniature room to visualise the character's changing environment.

Click the image to zoom in.

Development

Once we established our concept, we divided the responsibilities within the group, with me taking on the role of video editor. Together, we developed the script, outlined the individual scenes and created a list of the equipment and materials needed for production. We then worked on building the set, preparing the character, capturing the stop-motion photographs and creating the sound design.

During the final stage, I selected and organised the photographs, placing them in the correct sequence and adjusting the timing to bring the animation together. I also incorporated the sound design and worked closely with a more experienced teammate who provided additional editing support and guidance.

Results

The final result was a stop-motion short film combining handmade sets, character animation and sound design. Alongside the film, I also created an interactive teaser poster, which was for another subject within the course with a more horror-inspired visual direction.

Reflections

With no previous filmmaking experience, this project pushed me outside my comfort zone. I learned most through hands-on experience, asking questions and collaborating with my teammates. It gave me a first introduction to video editing and showed me how much I enjoy working with audiovisual storytelling.
